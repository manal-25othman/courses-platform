1. Signing in

  1. Open the address the school gave you.
  2. Type your username and password.
  3. Choose Sign in.
Capital letters in your username do not matter. Teacher, teacher and TEACHER all reach the same account. Your password is different: every capital in it counts.

Checking what you typed

Every password box has an eye at its right-hand edge. Press it to read what you typed, and press it again to hide it. The box is never revealed on its own, and what you typed is not lost when you look at it.

2. Finding your way around

Four places, always along the top:

WhereWhat it holds
DashboardWho needs a look at today.
Class progressEvery student, and how far each has gone.
StudentsAdding accounts, resetting passwords.
CurriculumYour units, and everything inside them.

The speech-bubble icon beside your name is your messages. Your name opens your own settings. Sign out is at the far end.

3. The dashboard

This is the first screen after you sign in, and it answers one question: who should I look at today?

  • Worth a look — students who have not started, or who have stopped.
  • This class — average progress, how many have finished, how many have not begun.
  • By unit — which units the class is moving through.
  • Everyone — the whole class, newest work first.

Choose any student's name to open her own page.

4. Your course and its units

Curriculum lists every unit, with what each one holds.

Units sit in two groups:

  • Course units — these count towards a student's progress.
  • Extra practice — offered to students, but not counted.

Choose a unit's name to open it. Inside, four tabs follow the order a student meets them: Vocabulary, Grammar, Activity, Unit test.

5. Draft and Published — the one rule that matters

If you read only one section, read this one.

Two things must both be true before a student sees anything. The item must be Published, and its unit must be visible. Miss either and she sees nothing.
WordMeaning
DraftSaved, and not visible to students. Safe to work on.
PublishedReleased — visible, once the unit is visible too.
Hidden from studentsThe whole unit is closed, whatever is published inside it.
Saving never publishes anything. You can correct a draft all day and no student sees a word of it. But in a unit that is already visible, a change to an already-published item is live straight away. To work privately on a live unit, hide the unit first.

6. Words

The Vocabulary tab holds the unit's words: the English word, its Arabic meaning, and a picture or a recording where you have added one.

  1. Open the unit and stay on Vocabulary.
  2. Add the English word and its Arabic meaning.
  3. Add a picture or your own recording if you want one.
  4. Save. It is a draft until you publish it.
A word with no Arabic meaning cannot be used in a game. Memory Match and Quick Match pair a word with its meaning, so a word missing one is left out of both.

7. Grammar

The Grammar tab holds the unit's grammar pages — an explanation, examples, and a picture of the page from the book where you have added one.

8. Activity questions

The Activity tab is where you write the questions students practise on. Each one can be published, edited or left as a draft on its own.

Try it as a student at the top shows exactly what a student would be asked, shuffled as she would see it. It records nothing.

9. The unit test

The Unit test tab works like the Activity tab, but its questions are kept for the test. A student does not meet them while practising.

10. Releasing a unit to your students

  1. Open the unit and check each tab holds what you want.
  2. Publish the items you want released. They can be published one at a time.
  3. Choose Review and publish… on the unit.
  4. Read what it tells you is not ready, and fix anything you want to fix.
  5. Make the unit visible.

On the Curriculum screen the unit's chip changes from Hidden from students, and the count at the top of the page — open to students — goes up by one. That count is the quickest check that a release worked.

11. Games and Grammar Adventure

There are three games. They are for practice and enjoyment: nothing a student does in a game is recorded — no progress moves, no score is kept, no test attempt is spent.

GameWhat it playsNeeds
Memory MatchThe unit's words and meanings.6 complete words
Quick MatchThe unit's words and meanings.4 complete words
Grammar AdventureThe unit's grammar questions.3 usable questions

Grammar Adventure is made from your questions

The game has no content of its own. It plays the published grammar questions from the unit's Activity tab — so writing those questions is what switches the game on. The Activity tab tells you where the unit stands:

Only questions a student can answer by tapping are counted. Ordering words, true or false, and multiple choice tied to a grammar page. Questions that need a typed answer — spelling, for instance — are left out, because typing is not a way through a world. If you are one short, that is usually why.

12. Your students

Students is where accounts are made and looked after.

Adding a student

  1. Choose Add a student.
  2. Type her full name and a username.
  3. Set a first password and write it down before you leave the screen.
  4. Save.
The first password is temporary, and shown once. She is asked to choose her own the first time she signs in. You cannot read it again afterwards — nobody can, not even the school's administrator. If it is lost, reset it.
Two usernames cannot differ only in capitals. If sara exists, Sara is refused — because the two would be the same name to a student trying to sign in.

Resetting a password

  1. Open the student on the Students screen.
  2. Choose to reset her password.
  3. Give her the new one. She is asked to change it when she next signs in.

13. Watching progress

Class progress shows every student and how far each has gone.

Choose a student to open her own page: which words she has learnt, which grammar pages she has read, how her activities and tests went, and when she last worked. You can write to her from the same page.

14. Messages

The speech bubble at the top right is your messages. A number on it means something is waiting. It checks for new messages roughly every two minutes.

Choose it to see which students are waiting, and choose a student to open her conversation. Messages stay inside the platform.

15. Your WhatsApp number

Your settings — behind your name at the top — hold a WhatsApp number.

  1. Open your settings from your name at the top.
  2. Type the number including the country code, for example +966 5x xxx xxxx.
  3. Save.
Nothing is shown until you set it. While the box is empty, no WhatsApp button appears anywhere and no number exists in the platform. Your students see the button only once you have set a number, and only your own students see it. Clearing the box takes it down again.

16. Passwords — yours and theirs

If you forget yours

  1. Choose Forgot your password? on the sign-in screen.
  2. Type the e-mail address on your account.
  3. Follow the link that arrives. It expires, and works once.

If your account has no e-mail address, ask your school's administrator to reset it for you.

If a student forgets hers

Reset it from the Students screen — see section 12.

17. Signing out, and being signed out

Sign out is at the top right. Use it on a shared staffroom machine.

You are asked before you are signed out. After twenty minutes with nothing happening, a box appears and counts down for a minute. Choose Stay signed in and nothing is lost. It is there for the tablet left on a desk with a register still on the screen.

18. When something goes wrong

A student says she cannot see a unit you published.

Check both halves: the item must be Published and the unit must not be Hidden from students. On the Curriculum screen, the open to students count at the top tells you how many units are actually released.

She cannot sign in.

Capitals in her username do not matter, so that is not the cause. Check the username is spelled right, and reset her password from the Students screen. If she has just changed her password, she has to sign in again with the new one — that is normal.

Grammar Adventure is missing, or says it needs more.

Open the unit's Activity tab and read the panel at the top. It needs three questions a student can answer by tapping. Spelling and other typed questions do not count towards it.

A word is missing from Memory Match or Quick Match.

Both games pair a word with its Arabic meaning. A word with no meaning cannot be paired, so it is left out.

You changed a published item and students saw it immediately.

That is how a visible unit behaves. To work on a live unit privately, hide the unit first, make your changes, then make it visible again.

The screen says your session has ended.

Sign in again. If it keeps happening, tell the school's administrator — an account that has been deactivated, or a school that has been closed, ends sessions straight away.

19. Questions people ask

Do games count towards a student's progress?

No. Nothing in a game is recorded — not progress, not a score, not a test attempt. They are for practice and enjoyment.

Can I see a student's password?

No, and neither can anyone else. Passwords are not stored in a readable form. If one is lost, reset it.

Can another teacher see my class?

No. You see your own students. Your school's administrator sees the school. No-one outside your school sees anything at all.

Does saving publish my work?

Never. Saving and publishing are separate, deliberately.

Can I work on my phone?

Yes. Every screen works on a phone, though the unit editor is easier on a larger screen.

What happens to a student's work if I delete her account?

Ask before you do it. Removing an account is not the same as correcting a mistake in a name, which you can edit directly.
